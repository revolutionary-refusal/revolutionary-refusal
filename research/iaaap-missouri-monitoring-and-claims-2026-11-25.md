# IAAAP — cross-state monitoring, exposure benchmarks and claims guidance
2026-11-25, pre-event review. Public accountability research; not a safety advisory.

## New findings
- IAI-350: Illinois EPA page updated November 25 at 5:15 a.m. CT, 57 minutes after the previously recorded 4:18 update. It lists another Army forecast release at 5 a.m. and retains the forecast of no Illinois plume impact. This is a forecast, not validated environmental results. EPA site 17211 still displayed November 24 as its latest dated profile update when checked. No outcome established.
- IAI-351: Missouri governor announced a multi-agency monitoring plan November 20 and confirmed state emergency operations center activation November 24. Missouri DNR identifies ten public surface-water systems with thirteen intakes within its stated monitoring vicinity and reports contacting all ten systems. Contact is not proof every intake has matched baseline and follow-up sampling. Its agriculture section expressly acknowledges potential lack of pre-event soil data and identifies fee-based soil/feed and veterinary testing. EPA separately reports federal pre-event sampling; this does not resolve Missouri farm-specific coverage or cost reimbursement. State agencies, water utilities and federal EPA are distinct evidence custodians.
- IAI-352: November 24 Missouri governor release compares anticipated public lead exposure to an OSHA occupational limit. OSHA's general-industry limit is 50 micrograms/m3 over eight hours; EPA's ambient lead standard is 0.15 micrograms/m3 on a rolling three-month basis. The numerical ratio (333.33) is NOT a risk ratio, and the different averaging periods/populations prevent direct comparison or a conclusion about incident-specific compliance. CDC's July 29, 2026 guidance distinguishes its child blood-lead reference value from a safety threshold. Missouri states expected risk is low and recommends precautionary measures. No evidence here establishes actual exposure or an unlawful standard.
- IAI-353: Missouri DNR claims FAQ gives a two-year period from the incident and names both FTCA and Military Claims Act. The federal statutory text in 28 USC 2401(b) and 10 USC 2733(b)(1) instead generally starts the two-year presentation period at claim accrual. The state wording is a simplified public summary, not proof a claim was denied or a deadline extended. The Military Claims Act has separate eligibility restrictions; do not assume both routes apply or allow duplicate recovery. Obtain written Army clarification rather than advising delay.
