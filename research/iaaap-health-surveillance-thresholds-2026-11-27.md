# IAAAP — public health surveillance thresholds, 27 November 2026

EPA incident register still lists eight documents; no newly validated environmental laboratory concentrations retrieved. Iowa HHS's 25 November 2026 12:30 p.m. advisory describes low inhalation risk based on information then available, pending soil results. This does not establish absence of exposure.

New documentary crosscheck: Iowa's annual under-six surveillance page (last updated 22 November 2026) describes a current CDC reference value of 3.5 µg/dL but defines its published confirmed-case measure at 10 µg/dL. Its source is HHLPSS. It also reports approximately 25% of under-six children tested annually statewide, and explains that the nonrandom data may not represent all children or identify exposure sources. These are statewide figures, not incident-specific coverage. Iowa's adopted rule ARC 8070C, filed 24 May 2024, effective 17 July 2024, Appendix B, requires daily reporting for results at least 20 µg/dL in children under six or pregnant women, and weekly reporting of other blood lead results. Current HHS environmental reporting table corroborates those categories. CDC's 29 July 2026 communication explicitly rejects interpreting a reference value as a safety threshold. The 3.5, 10, and 20 thresholds serve different purposes; no threshold demonstrates absence of risk. The 10-based public measure cannot enumerate the 3.5–<10 cohort. No actual increase, misreporting, or incident-related illness established.

Countercheck: HHS EPI manual separately describes telephone reporting for persons under 16 at 20 µg/dL, while adopted rule and environmental table specify under six or pregnant women; clarification of operational scope is warranted, not a finding of noncompliance. Laboratory data and public reporting are separate. No individual records sought.

Unsent records scope: Iowa HHS and local health authorities separately, existing privacy-protected aggregate counts by collection period and sufficiently broad geography, unique persons tested, confirmed results by consistent thresholds, capillary/venous definitions, reporting lag, suppression rules, and incident-specific surveillance protocols if any. Ask for clarification of the EPI manual and public dashboard metadata. EPA separately: validated environmental results, cutoffs, and revisions. Do not seek names, addresses, medical histories, or small-cell data. Clinical data cannot establish source without additional investigation.
