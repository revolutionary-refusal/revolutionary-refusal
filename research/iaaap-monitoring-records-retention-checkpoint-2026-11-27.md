# IAAAP — permit-specific monitoring record preservation
27 November 2026

EPA's emergency permit effective 13 November 2026, section II.L.1–4 (printed page 6, visually checked), requires representative monitoring, retention of underlying monitoring and calibration records, continuous instrument originals, required reports and supporting permit data for at least three years from the individual record's applicable date, subject to EPA extension. Groundwater monitoring records and associated elevations must be retained for the active life of the facility. Analytical data must meet the project quality objectives. For a sample collected 25 November 2026, the nominal three-year checkpoint is 25 November 2029; later records have later checkpoints. This is a minimum, not authorization to destroy records, and differs from the previously documented procurement financial retention rules. Permit expiration does not terminate this duty.

EPA response site 17211 still lists eight documents and its latest profile entry is dated 26 November. Illinois EPA's incident page still points to the federal dashboard. EPA's 26 November release reports preliminary airborne lead nondetection and expects initial post-event laboratory results as early as 27 November, not a guaranteed publication date. No new validated concentrations retrieved.

Unsent records scope: Army environmental records custodian and EPA Region 7 separately: anonymized sample inventory, dates, qualifiers, reporting limits, calibration and maintenance records, data quality acceptance and version history, and non-sensitive documentation of retention or extension decisions under II.L.2; state-held comparison copies independently where available.
