# IAAAP — competition approval and monitoring checkpoint, November 25, 2026

## New primary-source findings
1. The White House November 25 release reproduces EPA Administrator's statement of continued airborne lead nondetection more than five hours after the operation. This extends the one-hour official EPA checkpoint. It is an attributed agency statement, not independent measurements, a final safety determination or proof of no soil/water contamination. The same release compares particulate emissions with a fireworks event; methodology, units and lead-specific comparator data were not supplied. Do not treat this as a validated exposure comparison.
2. FAR 6.304(a)(2), (d), effective March 13, 2026: proposed awards above $900,000 and up to $20 million ordinarily require written approval by the designated competition advocate or specified higher official; estimated options count in the approval threshold. Secondary sources report PIKA award W519TC26CA049 on July 2, $770,810 obligated and $9,999,999 potential. If that estimated value represents the proposed contract including options, the approval tier is determined by that value, not the initially obligated amount. Executed contract and approval remain unverified. No new amount added to prior verified subtotal.
3. FAR 6.305: ordinary publication is within 14 days of award; urgency under 6.302-2 allows 30 days. For July 2 these are nominal July 16 and August 1, 2026 respectively. Public posting ordinarily need remain available only 30 days; security exception in (f) can remove publication obligation. FAR 6.302-6 concerns national security, not urgency. A secondary aggregator inconsistently describes urgency while citing 6.302-6. Actual authority and any security exception remain unknown. Written justification is required under both authorities. Failure to retrieve it now is not evidence of late publication, improper award or misconduct.
