# IAAAP — EPCRA source-water notification and independent custody
27 November 2026 (UTC observation on 28 November 2026).

New statutory crosswalk: 42 USC 11004(c) requires written follow-up after a qualifying reportable release; subsection (e), added 23 October 2018, requires the state emergency response commission to forward notice and follow-up to the drinking-water primacy agency, which must promptly forward notice to potentially affected community water systems. EPA's AWIA guidance corroborates this. Section 11044 separately requires public availability of follow-up notices during normal working hours and annual local newspaper notice of availability. These are independent recipient records, not merely EPA dashboard copies. EPA guidance says 30 days should suffice for follow-up but states may impose shorter periods; 25 December 2026 is an illustrative 30-day checkpoint for a qualifying 25 November release, not a demonstrated statutory due date. Distinguish the previously documented Iowa rule 105.5 from federal EPCRA.
Counterevidence: 40 CFR 355.31 exempts federally permitted releases and exposure solely on-site, among others. 42 USC 9601(10)(E) has specific permit and compliance criteria; an emergency treatment permit is not by itself proof that any actual release satisfies them. The amount treated is not the amount released. No reportable release, exemption determination, missing notice, or noncompliance established. EPA 26 November update reports preliminary air nondetection; EPA incident register still lists eight documents and linked dashboard returned no readable data in this retrieval. No new validated concentrations retrieved. Prior water-sampling and Missouri water-monitoring notes reviewed; this adds the statutory transmission/public access route, not a duplicate of sampling counts.
