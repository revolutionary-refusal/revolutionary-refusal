# IAAAP — compliance reporting and public records custody
26 November 2026. New accountability checkpoints, not an allegation of noncompliance.

The 13 November emergency permit (printed p. 4) incorporates applicable 40 CFR Part 270 requirements as of issuance. Section 270.30(l)(5) requires compliance/progress reporting within 14 days after each compliance-schedule date. Combining this with permit I.A's nominal 18 November deadline yields a nominal 2 December 2026 follow-up reporting checkpoint, subject to applicability, modification and any already accepted filing. The current eCFR text was verified; the exact historical snapshot was not accessible. This is a separate checkpoint from the underlying exposure-analysis submission, not a finding of missed compliance.

Printed p. 7, II.M.4–5, separately requires oral notice of qualifying noncompliance within 24 hours of awareness and written reporting within five days (EPA may waive in favor of 15 days), and correction of omitted or incorrect permit/report facts within 24 hours of awareness. No triggering noncompliance or incorrect Army filing is established. EPA's changing aggregate sampling counts do not themselves establish an Army reporting violation.

40 CFR 270.30(m) provides discretionary authority for an information repository. Ask for any determination, public-access plan, and versioned downloadable environmental data; the existence of the dashboard does not prove a formal repository designation or independently preserved records. EPA's 26 November update is still the latest retrieved primary environmental update. The site document list remains at eight documents; validated lab results were not retrieved. Army and EPA should be queried separately for receipt and acknowledgment dates, waiver/modification records, and any repository designation. No inquiry was submitted. Public aggregate records only; no sensitive operational details or personal identifiers.
