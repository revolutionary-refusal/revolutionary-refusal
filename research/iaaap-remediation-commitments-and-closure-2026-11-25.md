# IAAAP remediation commitments and closure accountability — 2026-11-25

## Findings
- Iowa governor's November 25 statement confirms operation completion and continuing state air, soil and water monitoring. Its nondetection statement expressly relies on EPA, so it is not independent laboratory confirmation.
- The Army's November 24 announcement promises government-funded restoration for residential properties testing above 200 ppm and immediate processing/payment of vibration-damage claims. These are public commitments, not evidence of approved claims, legal entitlement or actual payments.
- EPA's October 16, 2025 residential-soil directive sets 200 ppm as a screening level and 600 ppm as a regional removal management level; it supersedes January 2024 guidance. The latter recommended 200 ppm for screening and 100 ppm where additional exposure sources were identified. Screening and removal-management levels are different from site-specific cleanup requirements. The Army's public 200 ppm commitment should be tracked separately, not silently replaced with the national 600 ppm figure. 600/200 = 3, a comparison of different categories, not a measured exposure or a universal safety limit.
- EPA's November 13, 2026 emergency permit, pp. 16–18, requires short-term cleanup, closure certification, long-term planning and a final report. Conditions V.C–F require a Class 1 modification of the 2018 permit with prior Director approval in specified unfinished-cleanup or pending-approval circumstances. V.G requires the final report by permit expiration (February 11, 2027), including validated laboratory data and coverage of the September 29 storage permit. This is a continuity mechanism, not evidence that cleanup is complete. Prior ledger IAI-342 already recorded the 24-hour, five-day, 90-day and final-report deadlines; those are not new findings.
- The EPA response site's linked QAPP could not be retrieved through the web reader (content length 77,808,400 bytes exceeded its limit). No finding about QAPP contents or compliance is made. Permit pages 16–18 were visually inspected, but the original bytes were not independently archived.
