# Federal Post-Quantum Cryptography Readiness — GAO Audit

**Source:** GAO-27-108740, *Quantum Computing: Federal Actions Needed to Prepare for Emerging Cyber Threat* (October 6, 2026; errata October 14, 2026).

**Scope:** 24 Chief Financial Officers Act agencies; audit performed February 2024–September 2025. This October 2026 publication is a public, redacted version of sensitive report GAO-25-107392SU (September 11, 2025). These are audit-period findings, not verified current readiness.

## Documented findings

- **0/24** agencies fully addressed all three post-quantum cryptography (PQC) transition preparatory practices.
- **1/24** fully addressed the complete priority-system inventory activity; **22/24** partially addressed it and **1/24** had no inventory.
- **23/24** lacked a documented cryptography inventory maintenance process.
- **19/24** did not use automated cryptographic discovery tools.
- **0/24** fully addressed funding assessment practices; **21/24** partially addressed them and **3/24** did not address them.
- **0/24** had tested PQC in their own environments under the assessed practice; one had identified candidate vendor implementations for testing.
- **$7.1 billion** government-wide migration/replacement estimate reported by OMB, based on rough agency estimates and incomplete inventories—not a validated final cost.
- **89 recommendations** made to CISA and 23 CFO Act agencies in the September 2025 sensitive report.
- **10 agencies** determined some information in the sensitive report needed to be withheld from the public version.

## Why it matters

Public-key cryptography used for confidentiality and authentication may eventually be vulnerable to a cryptographically relevant quantum computer (CRQC). GAO identifies a **harvest-now, decrypt-later** risk. It also states today's quantum computers cannot break this cryptography, and expert timelines remain uncertain.

## Evidence and limitations

- GAO-27-108740, highlights (PDF p. 2); methodology (pp. 7–9, 42–48); inventory findings (pp. 27–33); funding (pp. 34–36); testing (pp. 37–38); recommendations (pp. 39–40).
- The agency-specific sensitive findings and original comment letters are not reproduced in this public version.
- The findings establish readiness gaps at the audit date, **not** a successful quantum attack or intentional misconduct.
