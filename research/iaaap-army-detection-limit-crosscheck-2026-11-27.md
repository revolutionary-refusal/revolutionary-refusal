# IAAAP — Army advisory detection threshold crosscheck
27 November 2026

New primary-source evidence: the Army public advisory issued 24 November 2026, page 2 (visually checked), labels its modeled airborne concentration contour 0.18 µg/m³ as the lead air sampling method detection limit. EPA's national ambient lead standard is 0.15 µg/m³ on a rolling three-month basis. The nominal values differ by 0.03 µg/m³ (20%), but their sampling periods, purposes and methods are not established as equivalent. This is NOT a regulatory exceedance, a health threshold for an individual brief exposure, or proof that the EPA Xact monitors use this limit. Historical Xact 625 quantitation figures from the previous note do not establish actual field settings either.

EPA's 26 November release reported preliminary airborne nondetection and anticipated laboratory results as early as 27 November. The incident register still displayed eight documents when checked; no new validated laboratory concentrations were retrieved. Absence from this register does not prove nonpublication elsewhere.

PROVEN: Army's published advisory legend and EPA's ambient standard, preliminary statement and register count. SUSPECTED: different public products may use distinct reporting conventions. UNVERIFIED: actual instrument-specific thresholds, data qualifiers, averaging basis, paired lab measurements, regulatory compliance and exposure outcomes.

Unsent records scope: ask EPA Region 7 and the Army separately for the analytical method and averaging interval underlying the advisory's 0.18 figure, its relationship (if any) to actual field instruments and dashboard nondetect encoding, aggregate quality-qualified observations, and laboratory validation. Request independently retained copies and review from state environmental agencies. No private addresses or sensitive operational information requested.
