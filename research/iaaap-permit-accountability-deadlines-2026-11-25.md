# IAAAP — permit accountability and deadlines, 2026-11-25

Pre-event accountability note; not a safety advisory. No completed treatment or post-event contamination verified in this review. Builds on research/iaaap-procurement-chronology-2026-11-25.md and research/iaaap-epa-17211-records-baseline-2026-11-25.md.

## Verified findings
- EPA's November 24 update reports more than 1,001 pre-treatment air, land and water samples and independent EPA sampling. This is an agency-reported count, not independently validated results or the number of distinct sites. Iowa's November 24 emergency proclamation covers Des Moines, Lee and Van Buren counties; it authorizes state resources but states no spending total.
- The November 13 emergency permit, II.L.2 and II.N.2 (pp. 6–7), establishes separate minimums of three years from each monitoring record's date and five years from permit effectiveness for the Operating Record (nominally November 13, 2031). Unresolved enforcement extends the latter; EPA may extend monitoring retention. Groundwater monitoring records are retained for the facility's active life. No destruction is authorized by these dates alone.
- Conditions IV.U.2.ii, IV.X and V.G (pp. 16–18) require specified soil and surface sampling within 24 hours of treatment, a laboratory report and proposed short-term remediation plan within five days of receiving validated data, a long-term plan within 90 days of EPA approving the closure report, and a final report by February 11, 2027, subject to applicable permit changes. Private property sampling in the specified area depends on rights of entry.
- Appendix A records financial responsibility Section 14 submitted September 18 and EPA comments on October 21 (33 elapsed days); amended application October 28. The actual financial instrument, if any, is unverified. 40 CFR 264.140(c) exempts federal and state governments from Subpart H financial requirements; this is not a blanket cleanup exemption or proof of contractor exemption. The emergency permit names the Army as permittee; EPA's December 2024 enforcement announcement identifies American Ordnance LLC as plant operator and a $39,311 joint penalty resolution of earlier alleged violations. Neither the penalty nor contractor relationship establishes responsibility for the current incident.
- Appendix B requires the conservative emission factor 1.00 for public-notification modeling. The previously noted 0.26 label is not yet independently authenticated here; if it denotes a comparable factor, it is 74% lower, not a measured reduction in exposure. Permit p. 3 explains uncertainty and the reason for the conservative factor. No substitution or permit breach is established.
- Eighth Circuit No. 26-3374, November 23, pp. 4, 7–9: stay denied; the Army was not a defendant, and the court expressly did not decide the separate emergency-exclusion course of conduct. The ruling is not a final merits determination or proof of zero harm.

## Oversight and records targets (unsent)
1. EPA Region 7: Section 14 versions, October 21 comments and disposition, November 18 nominal exposure-pathway resubmission and receipt, approved quality plan, anonymized sample identifiers/results/validation records, final report, and retention extensions.
2. Army contracting office: non-sensitive procurement requisition, hazard-notification dates, MuniRem award and nonexercise/cancellation instruments, payment and credit ledger, contractor responsibility clauses, insurance/indemnity and cleanup cost allocations. No operational or security-sensitive details sought.
3. Iowa DNR/HSEM: independent sampling and validation, state response costs, reimbursement claims and grant/appropriation identifiers; reconcile with federal records without duplicating payments.

PROVEN: published terms, agency-reported counts and procedural ruling. SUSPECTED: possible gaps between public modeling and notification records requiring comparison, not misconduct. UNVERIFIED: actual contamination, sample results, costs, claims, contractor liability, financial assurance terms and award cancellation amounts. Independent EPA and state checks are useful but do not establish independent archival custody. Earlier procurement and later hazard recognition are not inherently inconsistent.
