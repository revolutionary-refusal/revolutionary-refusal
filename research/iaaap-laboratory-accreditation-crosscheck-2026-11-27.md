# IAAAP — laboratory accreditation crosscheck
27 November 2026

EPA's October 2026 NLLAP directory identifies recognition by sample matrix (paint, soil, dust wipes) and five accrediting organizations. The 23 October 2024 Federal Register notice (89 FR 84576, document 2024-24558, docket EPA-HQ-OPPT-2023-0456) establishes LQSR 4.0, with the transition from 3.0 ending 22 December 2025. Recognition requires proficiency testing (quarterly rounds) and an accreditation audit. This provides a way to check claims independently with accreditation bodies, not just the incident dashboard. The notice is independently retrievable from GPO. The program is principally for residential lead-based paint testing; its applicability to this emergency response is not established, and it does not certify incident air or water results. An accreditation credential is not validation of any particular sample.

EPA's 26 November announcement expected initial laboratory results as early as 27 November. The incident register still lists eight documents; public dashboard redirects to an ArcGIS application without readable data in this retrieval. No new validated laboratory concentrations retrieved. Missouri's 25 November advisory supports the preliminary airborne finding but does not establish independent laboratory validation.

PROVEN: published accreditation framework, transition dates and directory scope. SUSPECTED: dependence on a single public reporting channel can impede independent review. UNVERIFIED: participating laboratories, applicable methods/accreditation scope, sample-level validation, results, and custody. No allegation of contamination or noncompliance.

Unsent records scope: EPA Region 7, Army, and state agencies separately: laboratory identities and applicable certificates/scopes as of sample analysis, proficiency and quality-control summaries, chain-of-custody and deidentified aggregate paired data, laboratory reporting and validation dates, public release/revision log, and whether NLLAP or another accreditation scheme applies to each matrix. Obtain corroboration from applicable accrediting bodies. Exclude private addresses and operational details.
