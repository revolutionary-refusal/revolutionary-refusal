# F-35 Parts Accountability — GAO Evidence Note

Date reviewed: 2026-11-22
Source: GAO-27-108713, published 2026-11-09, https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-108713
Full accessible report: https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-27-108713/index.html

## PROVEN (GAO findings)
- Approximately 26 million F-35 parts, valued at approximately $31 billion, at more than 700 contractor- and government-managed facilities.
- DOD's accountable property records were incomplete, inaccurate, and not updated in real time.
- As of March 2026, the F-35 Joint Program Office did not know the status of more than $85 million of lost F-35 parts previously reported by GAO in 2023.
- Physical inventories did not verify the completeness of all F-35 parts. In March 2026, DOD requested Defense Contract Audit Agency assistance establishing an auditable baseline; as of June 2026 the first full inventory target was March 2027.
- JPO had not established effective procedures for monitoring and analyzing all reported part losses.

## LIMITS / COUNTEREVIDENCE
- $31 billion is approximate total value of parts, NOT the amount missing or stolen.
- More than $85 million represents lost-part cases with unresolved status as described by GAO, NOT proven criminal theft, established restitution debt, or a verified amount still unrecovered today.
- GAO records also document remedial work under way; failure of current controls does not establish intent.

## ACCOUNTABILITY QUESTIONS
1. Obtain JPO loss reports and reconciliations for the >$85M identified in 2023; distinguish located, written off, contractor-liable, reimbursed, and unresolved items.
2. Request current APSR reconciliation and DCAA inventory plan, including March 2027 milestone.
3. Identify which facilities and contractors hold assets tied to unresolved losses; do not allege culpability without transaction-level evidence.
4. Cross-check GAO-25-106868 contractor-acquired property recommendations: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-106868

## INDEPENDENCE / QA
Primary GAO report checked against its accessible version. Do not equate inventory deficiencies with fraud. Maintain this as a separate evidence note, not a restitution entry, until a legally established debt or recovery is documented.
