# IAAAP — contractor claims and cost allocation
27 November 2026

EPA's incident register still lists eight documents; the public data dashboard redirects to a JavaScript application that returned no readable data in this retrieval. The 26 November EPA update reported preliminary airborne nondetections and anticipated laboratory results beginning as early as 27 November. No new validated concentrations were retrieved; this does not mean none exist.

Three legally distinct financial pathways must be reconciled rather than treating all claims as federal debts:

- 28 USC 2671 excludes federal contractors from its definition of a federal agency. The Army's November 2026 claims guide addresses Army-related injury and property damage; it does not establish contractor liability or entitlement.
- FAR 52.228-7 provides for approved insurance costs and qualifying uninsured third-party liabilities in contracts containing the clause, subject to exclusions, appropriations, and written settlement approval or final judgments. DFARS 228.311-1 prescribes its use for qualifying cost-reimbursement contracts unless waived.
- FAR 52.250-1 and 50.104-1 establish a separate conditional indemnification mechanism for contract-defined unusually hazardous risks. DFARS Part 250 identifies approval and recordkeeping mechanisms. Neither a hazardous activity nor a government-owned plant automatically establishes an indemnity.

EPA identified the Army as owner and American Ordnance as operator in its December 2024 enforcement release. No executed incident-specific insurance or indemnity clause, approval, waiver, insurer payment, settlement, or allocation to American Ordnance or Day & Zimmermann was obtained. The September 2026 permit financial-responsibility submission previously logged is a separate regulatory record, not proof of contractual indemnity.
