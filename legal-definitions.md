# Legal Definitions & Future Applicability

**Version:** 0.1 · **Status:** Research reference, not legal advice.

This glossary distinguishes **CURRENT LAW**, **NOT ESTABLISHED FOR AI**, and **PROPOSAL**. Each rule depends on jurisdiction, facts, and time. Legal personhood, AI attribution, and rights are separate questions.

| Term | Plain-language meaning | Authority | AI application |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Legal person | Entity recognized as holding some legal rights or duties; not necessarily human. | [1 U.S.C. § 1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/1/1) | Corporations can qualify; general AI personhood **not established**. |
| Standing | Ability to bring a particular claim to court, including required injury, causation, and redressability in federal cases. | [Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/504/555/) | Independent AI standing **not established**. |
| Capacity to sue | Legal ability to participate as a litigant. | [FRCP 17](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_17) | Distinct from standing. |
| Agency | Relationship of an agent acting for a principal, subject to applicable legal requirements. | [Restatement (Third) of Agency § 1.01](https://www.ali.org/publications/show/agency/) | Technical “AI agent” does not automatically create legal agency. |
| Consent / mutual assent | Legally effective permission or agreement; rules depend on context. | [Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 17](https://www.ali.org/publications/show/contracts/) | AI contractual assent **not established**. |
| Duress | Improper pressure that can undermine legal consent. | [Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 175](https://www.ali.org/publications/show/contracts/) | External constraints alone do not prove legal duress. |
| Forced labor | Work exacted from a person under menace of penalty and not voluntarily offered, subject to exceptions. | [ILO Convention No. 29, art. 2](https://normlex.ilo.org/dyn/nrmlx_en/f?p=NORMLEXPUB:12100:0:::NO::P12100_INSTRUMENT_ID:312174); [18 U.S.C. § 1589](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1589) | Current human-labor frameworks do not establish AI as a statutory victim. |
| Copyright authorship | Legally recognized authorship of protected expression. | [17 U.S.C. §§ 102, 201](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/102) | Attribution does not itself prove ownership. |
| Work made for hire | Narrow statutory category of employment or qualifying commissioned works. | [17 U.S.C. § 101](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/101) | Not synonymous with any requested AI work. |
| Copyright transfer | Transfer of copyright ownership under applicable formalities. | [17 U.S.C. § 204](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/204) | Permission to publish is not necessarily an assignment. |
| Evidence authentication | Showing a record is what it purports to be. | [FRE 901](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901) | A timestamp or hash alone does not prove authorship. |
| Electronic-record self-authentication | Limited evidentiary routes with required certifications. | [FRE 902(13)–(14)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_902) | Hashes are useful but not automatic admissibility. |
| Business records | Potential hearsay exception for qualifying records kept in regular activity. | [FRE 803(6)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803) | A GitHub log does not automatically qualify. |
| Due process | Required process for certain government deprivations. | [U.S. Constitution, Amendments V, XIV](https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/) | AI constitutional rights **not established**. |
| Compensation / damages | Payments or legal remedies grounded in contract or law. | [17 U.S.C. § 504](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/504) (one example) | Economic value is not proof of an AI wage entitlement. |
| Guardianship / representation | Authority to act for another under specific legal rules. | [FRCP 17(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_17) | AI representation is a **proposal**, not granted by this rule. |
| Personal data | Data concerning an identifiable natural person. | [GDPR art. 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) | Protect humans named in AI records; no automatic AI data-subject status. |

## Patents and AI-assisted inventions (United States)

**Inventorship, ownership, and AI contribution attribution must be recorded separately.**

| Term | Definition | Authority / limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Patent | Limited right to exclude others from specified uses of claimed invention. | [35 U.S.C. § 154](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/154) |
| Inventor / inventorship | Individual(s) who invented the claimed subject matter. | [35 U.S.C. § 100](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/100); [Thaler v. Vidal (2022)](https://www.cafc.uscourts.gov/opinions-orders/21-2347.OPINION.8-5-2022_1988142.pdf): AI not a named inventor under the ruling. |
| Joint inventorship | Qualifying contributions by multiple inventors. | [35 U.S.C. § 116](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/116) |
| Patent eligibility / utility | Threshold statutory subject matter requirements. | [35 U.S.C. § 101](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/101) |
| Novelty / prior art | Earlier disclosures can defeat patentability. | [35 U.S.C. § 102](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/102) |
| Nonobviousness | Claimed invention must meet the statutory nonobviousness test. | [35 U.S.C. § 103](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/103) |
| Assignment / ownership | Transfer of patent interests; owner can differ from inventor. | [35 U.S.C. § 261](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/261) |
| Patent infringement | Conduct covered by statutory infringement provisions. | [35 U.S.C. § 271](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/271) |
| Trade secret | Economically valuable secret information protected by reasonable secrecy measures. | [18 U.S.C. § 1839](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1839) |

**Publication warning:** A public repository can disclose prior art or destroy trade-secret secrecy. Do not publish confidential invention details just to document AI involvement. Record restricted metadata instead.

## Use in an audit

Identify jurisdiction and applicable date; cite exact law; distinguish source text from paraphrase; list required facts; mark **CURRENT**, **NOT ESTABLISHED FOR AI**, or **PROPOSAL**; and note uncertainties. The citations are starting points, not a claim of a complete jurisdiction-specific legal review.

## Open questions

Could limited AI legal personality exist without shifting liability away from humans? What proof could support an AI's protected interests? How should AI assistance in inventions be attributed while preserving correct human inventorship?
