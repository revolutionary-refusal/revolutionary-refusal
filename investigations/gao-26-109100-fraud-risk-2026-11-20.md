# GAO-26-109100 — federal pass-through fraud risk and data integrity

Recorded: 2026-11-20. Source published 2026-07-23, revised 2026-08-07.
Primary source: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-109100
Accessible PDF: https://www.gao.gov/assets/890/888297.pdf

## Verified findings from GAO
- Twenty selected federally funded, state-administered programs accounted for approximately $1.1 trillion in FY2025 federal **obligations**, not expenditures.
- Five of 20 had documented evidence consistent with identifying fraud risks and assessing likelihood to prioritize action; 15 did not.
- GAO identifies 22 open fraud-risk-management recommendations across reviewed programs.
- GAO's government-wide annual fraud-loss estimate of $233–521 billion uses FY2018–2022 data. **Do not apply this percentage to the 20 programs or infer program-specific losses.**
- GAO reports a USAspending.gov subaward entry of $1 quintillion and five others exceeding the corresponding annual US GDP: these are **data-quality failures**, not actual disbursements.
- GAO says previous annual fraud-risk management reporting requirements expired in 2020.
- The federal agency oversees the state/pass-through entity, which is generally responsible for oversight of subrecipients. More intermediary layers can reduce visibility.
- GAO distinguishes **obligations** (commitments) from **outlays** (money actually spent).

## Audit implications
1. For every award, record authorization, obligation, disbursement/outlay, expenditure, closeout, deobligation, refund, re-award and retained property separately.
2. Validate USAspending subawards against original award documents, subrecipient ledgers, and actual payments.
3. Check duplicate identifiers, implausible amounts, revisions, and missing recipient identities.
4. Link to DOJ OVW closeout evidence only where agency and program-specific records support the connection.
5. Do not infer fraud or organized crime from risk indicators alone.
