### DOWIG-2027-024 — findings verified from full report (November 16, 2026)

- [Official 12-page report PDF](https://media.defense.gov/2026/Nov/19/2004030901/-1/-1/1/DOWIG-2027-024_FINAL_20261116_508%20SECURE.PDF) — user supplied original document; inspected 2026-11-20.
- **Finding:** 179 of 359 scrapped V-22 blades (49.9%) were still marked Ready for Issue in maintenance systems: 117 ACTS only, 37 NALCOMIS OOMA only, 25 both (printed p. 1).
- **Method:** Serial-number comparison against contractor scrap disposition records, ACTS as of May 2026 and OOMA as of June 2026; 20 blades in a nonstatistical sample of property loss cases plus 339 outside sample (printed p. 3).
- **Agency explanation:** ACTS introduced December 2025; officials said historical card migration/data-entry errors likely caused discrepancies; reported correcting ACTS statuses for scrapped blades (printed p. 2).
- **Management response:** V-22 JPO agreed with the recommendation to validate all parts records but disputed the interpretation of RFI status because Navy Enterprise Resource Planning is the authoritative inventory/supply system (printed pp. 4–5).
- **Status:** IG recommendation **open** pending evidence and verification of corrections; estimated completion date requested within 30 days (printed p. 4). No evidence in report that scrapped blades were installed or issued to aircraft. Potential broader inaccuracies involving other scrapped parts remain to be assessed.
- **Access:** Report says no classified or CUI information (printed p. 6). Keep original PDF URL and report number for durable retrieval.

## Federal information technology governance — Clinger–Cohen (1996)

- [GovInfo — Public Law 104-106, 110 Stat. 186 (February 10, 1996), complete 519-page enacted statute](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-104publ106/pdf/PLAW-104publ106.pdf) — **primary official source, opened and inspected for act identity and division structure**. National Defense Authorization Act for FY1996: Division D is Federal Acquisition Reform; Division E is Information Technology Management Reform. Use this as the authoritative 1996 enacted text when comparing with [Wikipedia's ITMRA overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Technology_Management_Reform_Act_of_1996). Full 519 pages not audited line by line; later amendments and current codification must be checked separately.

- [Information Technology Management Reform Act of 1996 — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Technology_Management_Reform_Act_of_1996) — secondary overview.
- [Public Law 104-106, Division E — Treasury-hosted full statute PDF](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/236/Clinger-Cohen_Act_of_1996.pdf) — primary enacted legislation, February 10, 1996; ITMRA together with Division D Federal Acquisition Reform Act subsequently named the Clinger–Cohen Act.
- [Department of Defense CIO legislative reference](https://dodcio.defense.gov/Portals/0/Documents/ciodesrefvolone.pdf) — federal CIO responsibilities and legislative history.
- [Congressional Research Service, *Access to Agency Data and Information*](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48423/R48423.3.pdf) — later analysis of federal IT governance and statutory changes.
- [Public Law 104-208 — statutory renaming](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/DownloadDocument?objectID=112375701) — September 30, 1996 amendment naming both Divisions D and E the Clinger–Cohen Act.

**Key provisions:** agency CIO accountability, capital planning and investment controls, OMB oversight, evaluation of technology procurement and performance, and assessment of government versus contractor performance of certain functions. **Research connection:** Goldwater–Nichols (1986) command organization; Clinger–Cohen (1996) federal IT management; Global Information Grid (1999 onward) military networking. These are distinct legal/policy developments, not proof of coordinated misconduct. Track current codification and later amendments separately from 1996 enacted text.
